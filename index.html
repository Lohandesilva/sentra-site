Where the margin goes

The work gets built on site. The paperwork gets chased at month-end.

Nobody means to give work away. It leaks out in the gap between the site and the claim.

1

Verbal instructions, nothing in writing

The builder's super says "while you're up there, put another circuit in" or "shift that duct run, the ceiling's changed". Everyone nods and nobody raises an SI. By claim time there's no instruction on file, no hours, and a builder's QS who'll ask for both.

2

Foremen are cooked by knock-off

A leading hand coming off a ten-hour day isn't opening an app or typing up a docket in the ute. Another login for the crew is another thing that doesn't get used. A phone call on the drive home does.

3

The office chases at month-end

The PM rings the foreman after claim cut-off and gets "I reckon about two days?". The variation goes in soft, goes in late, or gets knocked back. In Victoria you get one payment claim a month, so whatever misses the cut-off waits for the next one.

Unclaimed variations never show up as a line on the WIP. They show up as a job that finished tighter than the estimate said it would.
Built around your trade

The questions are different on an electrical job than on a fire job

The check-in call is scripted for the way each trade actually picks up extra work, so the foreman gets asked the right things in the right order.

Electrical

Extra rough-in and additional circuits, switchboard changes, points relocated after the ceiling grid or partitions move, and after-hours work to suit the builder's program.

HVAC

Extra duct runs and diffuser relocations after services coordination, FCU position changes, additional controls points, and re-balancing after the fitout shifts.

Fire

Head and detector relocations after ceiling changes, additional devices, FIP re-programming, and re-commissioning and re-testing for the certifier.

Hydraulic

Extra drops and floor wastes, relocated fixtures, additional pressure tests, rework after wet areas get redesigned, and out-of-sequence attendance.

Commercial Fitout

Partition and framing changes, plasterboard rework, extra ceilings and bulkheads, additional mobilisations, and weekend or after-hours work.

Three steps

No new software for your crews

Step 1

ERP job sync

Sentra reads your active jobs and assigned foremen from your ERP or job management system, so it knows who to ring and which job they're on.

Step 2

Foreman check-in

A short phone call at knock-off or on the drive home. A few fixed questions on extra work, hours, materials and who instructed it. Two or three minutes, voice only.

Step 3

Job record and office summary

Draft VO notes land on the job record. The PM, estimator or office gets a summary the same day, with any missing SI or written confirmation flagged for chasing.
